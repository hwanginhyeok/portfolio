Dear Apple Core Technology Operations Team,

My graduate research at Konkuk University's **Reliability-Based Design Optimization (RBDO) Lab** was not a side topic — it was the focus of my master's program. Over two years I worked on accelerated life testing, prognostics, and failure-mode characterization of electric drivetrains, contributing through my thesis on IPMSM fault-diagnosis simulation and a government-funded PHM SoC project. I received the 2022 Best Paper Award from the Korean Society for Reliability and contributed as co-author to three peer-reviewed publications (IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement · Journal of Power Electronics · Solar Energy, all 2024).

What distinguishes my profile is the bridge between that academic foundation and production reality. At GINT Corp. I led the GT-SS500 agricultural autonomous vehicle through APQP Phase 2–3 to a first-article production plan of 16 units, plus 2 transport carts and 2 spares. Concretely: DFMEA work on #201 and #210 (Step 1–7) yielded five AP=H findings, all closed before ramp; four root-cause analyses were closed (MCB electrolytic carbonization #204, LCD-mode state-machine bug #79, pump O-ring freeze, GND-bounce noise); 27 NCRs were tracked through to closure as a closed-loop corrective action process; and four custom test rigs were built in-house — dynamometer torque control (nonlinearity 0.008%), fan bench (airflow 7.9 → 12.4 m/s, +57%), pump bench, and bumper safety rig (stop distance 0.082 m at 3 km/h, peak current 308 A, settling time 467 ms). On the system-integration side, I designed a 5-node CAN distributed-control architecture with four DBC files and detected four breaking changes before integration through a three-pass consistency protocol. BOM management across 132 items surfaced 23 stock-shortage risks ahead of ramp. This combination — methodology depth plus production-stage execution — is what the Apple Core Technology Operations Reliability Engineer role asks for.

On the semiconductor side, I co-authored (third author) a programmable online bond-wire fault detection and location method for IGBT modules using inverter output parameters, published in IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement (2024, DOI 10.1109/TIM.2024.3472910). My collaboration on identifying failure modes in IPMSM under accelerated life testing through a dual-sensor architecture appeared in the Journal of Power Electronics (2024, sixth author, DOI 10.1007/s43236-024-00810-8). I also co-authored (fourth author) a study applying damage summation (Miner's Rule) to PV polymer lifetime prediction under continuously varying environments, published in Solar Energy (2024, DOI 10.1016/j.solener.2024.112645). The methodology overlap with camera-module ALT, multi-sensor fault diagnosis, and lifetime estimation under varying use profiles is what allows me to translate this background to optical-component reliability work.

The optics domain is the real gap in my background, and I want to be direct about it. Methodology, not domain familiarity, defines a reliability engineer's transferability. To close the gap, I completed a self-study brief covering VCM failure modes, ALT stress-factor mapping (HTOL, thermal cycling, drop, THB), DFMEA examples with RPN calculation, and PHM current-signature approaches for actuator health monitoring. The combination of graduate-level reliability methodology and production-stage execution is the foundation I would bring to Apple's component reliability work.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how this background maps to Apple's component reliability challenges in more detail.
