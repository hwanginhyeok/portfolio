Dear Apple Core Technology Operations Team,

My graduate research at Konkuk University's **Reliability-Based Design Optimization (RBDO) Lab** was not a side topic — it was the focus of my master's program. Over two years I worked on accelerated life testing, prognostics, and failure-mode characterization of electric drivetrains, contributing through my thesis on IPMSM fault-diagnosis simulation and a government-funded PHM SoC project. I received the 2022 Best Paper Award from the Korean Society for Reliability and contributed as co-author to three peer-reviewed publications (IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement · Journal of Power Electronics · Solar Energy, all 2024).

What distinguishes my profile is the bridge between that academic foundation and production reality. At GINT Corp. I led the GT-SS500 agricultural autonomous vehicle through APQP Phase 2–3 to a first-article production plan of 16 units, plus 2 transport carts and 2 spares. Concretely: I authored DFMEAs covering Step 1–7 of the AIAG-VDA process and resolved five high-priority risk items before production ramp; I closed four root-cause analyses on real field issues — MCB electrolytic carbonization (resolved by upgrading the breaker to an IEC 60947-2-grade part with re-qualification), an LCD-mode state-machine bug that allowed undefined transitions, a pump O-ring freeze in low-temperature operation, and ground-bounce noise on the control bus; I tracked 27 NCRs through to closure as a closed-loop corrective action process; and I built four custom test rigs in-house — dynamometer torque control (nonlinearity 0.008%), fan bench (airflow 7.9 → 12.4 m/s, +57%), pump bench, and bumper safety rig (stop distance 0.082 m at 3 km/h, peak current 308 A, settling time 467 ms). On the system-integration side, I designed a 5-node CAN distributed-control architecture with four DBC files and detected four breaking changes before integration through a three-pass consistency protocol. BOM management across 132 items surfaced 23 stock-shortage risks ahead of ramp. This combination — methodology depth plus production-stage execution — is what the Apple Core Technology Operations Reliability Engineer role asks for.

On the semiconductor side, I co-authored (third author) a programmable online bond-wire fault detection and location method for IGBT modules using inverter output parameters, published in IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement (2024, DOI 10.1109/TIM.2024.3472910). My collaboration on identifying failure modes in IPMSM under accelerated life testing through a dual-sensor architecture appeared in the Journal of Power Electronics (2024, sixth author, DOI 10.1007/s43236-024-00810-8). Both projects relied on the same core practice — defining failure modes, instrumenting ALT, and reading multi-sensor signals to locate degradation — which is the practice I would bring to component-reliability work at Apple.

The optics domain is the real gap in my background, and I want to be direct about it. I have not worked on cameras, VCMs, or lens assemblies; what I bring is graduate research in reliability and three years of production-stage execution on different components, and the working assumption that the same methodology — DFMEA, root-cause analysis, ALT, multi-sensor signal processing, NCR close-out — is what carries between domains. I would welcome the chance to discuss how that fits Apple's component reliability work in more detail.
