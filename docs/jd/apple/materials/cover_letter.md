Dear Apple Core Technology Operations Team,

My graduate research at Konkuk University's **Reliability-Based Design Optimization (RBDO) Lab** was not a side topic — it was the entire focus. Over two years, the lab's work centered on accelerated life testing, prognostics, and failure mode characterization of electric drivetrains. Reliability was the language of every meeting, every paper, and every experimental setup. That foundation earned a Best Paper Award from the Korean Society for Reliability in 2022 and contributed to three peer-reviewed publications (IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement · Solar Energy · Journal of Power Electronics, all 2024).

What distinguishes my profile is the bridge between that academic rigor and production reality. At GINT Corp., leading the GT-SS500 agricultural autonomous vehicle through APQP Phase 2–3 to first-article shipment (16 units) meant working inside the constraints that textbooks omit — BOM freezes, supplier NCRs, Gate Review deadlines, and line-stop decisions. Concretely: five DFMEAs authored per AIAG-VDA 2019 with AP=H items all resolved before ramp; four RCAs closed using 5 Why and Ishikawa diagrams (MCB electrolytic corrosion, state-machine safety fault, O-ring freeze, GND noise); NCR tracking across 27 items operated as a FRACAS-equivalent closed-loop corrective action process; and four test rigs built from zero aligned with MIL-STD-810 / IEC 60068-2 environmental test methods, including sealed CAN connectors qualified to IEC 60529 IP67. IQC/OQC acceptance criteria were defined consistent with Six Sigma DMAIC, with Cpk/Ppk monitored for supplier qualification. This combination — methodology depth plus mass production quality experience — is what the Reliability Engineer role at Apple's Core Technology Operations requires.

On the semiconductor side, I directly conducted Power Cycling Tests per JESD47/JEP122 on IGBT modules, identifying bond-wire lift-off as the primary failure mode (P-01, IEEE TIM 2024, DOI 10.1109/TIM.2024.3472910). The IPMSM ALT program (P-05, J. Power Electron. 2024) introduced me to the full design-test-redesign cycle through dual-sensor architecture failure characterization. Applied damage summation (Miner's Rule) for lifetime prediction under variable stress histories gave further grounding in data-driven reliability analysis (P-04, Solar Energy 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.solener.2024.112645). The same methodology — ALT design, Weibull analysis, multi-sensor fault diagnosis — applies directly to camera module, VCM, and lens reliability evaluation.

The optics domain is the only real gap in my background, and I want to be direct about it. Methodology, not domain familiarity, defines a reliability engineer's transferability. To prepare, I completed a self-study document covering VCM failure modes, ALT stress factor mapping (HTOL, thermal cycling, drop, THB), DFMEA examples with RPN calculation, and PHM current-signature approaches for actuator health monitoring. Apple's camera and sensor stack — the most reliability-demanding component assembly in a consumer product — is exactly where the combination of RBDO Lab methodology and APQP production experience can add value from day one.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how this background maps to Apple's component reliability challenges in more detail.
